The problem · Mars EVA gap

Working on Mars, seen from Earth

When explorers leave a Mars habitat, orbital maps are too coarse, GPS does not exist, and help on Earth is minutes away. Without live local 3D context, both the astronaut and Mission Control operate partially blind.

01
Delayed link

Signals between Mars and Earth HQ take minutes each way—crews need local AI at the Base and in the helmet.

02
Unknown terrain

No GPS network exists—orbital maps alone miss meter-scale rocks, crater slopes, and habitat interiors.

03
Limited crew view

Without live 3D mapping relayed through the Base, Earth HQ cannot follow explored zones or crew routes.

The solution · Three-tier architecture

Helmet, Martian Base, and Earth HQ

1
EVA Helmet Module

Captures 3D depth, color, and visor HUD. Keeps ASR & TTS always on; pauses its small LLM when paired with Base, or wakes it as fallback.

MISSION CONTROL · HUGE LLMs

Extra info · Engineering realism

Built for what can break on Mars

01
Optics & Dust
Martian dust & particle glare

Fine dust coats lenses and scatters light. The target EVA helmet protects compute inside a sealed housing and pairs optics with LiDAR and IR sensing.

02
Lighting Extremes
Harsh sunlight & crater shadow

Optical depth degrades in direct sun or pitch darkness. A glare-adaptive HUD pairs with a night-vision IR camera and IR / UV / visible lights.

03
Communications
Severed Base links & Earth lag

If terrain cuts the Base link, always-on ASR & TTS auto-wake the helmet's small LLM, notify the astronaut of fallback, and buffer scans for Base/HQ sync.

04
Power & Compute
Tiered compute saves suit battery

Pausing the helmet's small LLM during Base-assisted tasks saves power; Base runs medium/large LLMs, while Earth HQ runs huge engineering models.
